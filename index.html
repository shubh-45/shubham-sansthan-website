Who we are

A name Barmer district knows

Subham Sansthan has been registered under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958 since the year 2000, and is a familiar name across the Barmer region.

We are registered with the Public Relations Bureau under the Government of India's Ministry of Information & Broadcasting, and with the National AIDS Control Organisation in New Delhi. For more than seventeen years we have run HIV and AIDS awareness programmes here without a break. We have been honoured several times at district level for our contribution to social welfare. We worked on the national Aadhaar promotion campaign. And through the COVID-19 pandemic, we stayed in the field.

The work continues across the district today — in awareness camps, in schools and health centres, in the village square, and wherever a family needs help reaching what it is already entitled to.

What we do

Six things we keep turning up for

Our work follows the shape of the district: long distances, scarce water, and communities that have been reached last by almost everything.

HIV & AIDS awareness

Our longest-running programme. For more than seventeen unbroken years we have taken frank, plain-spoken information about HIV and AIDS into the villages of Barmer district, in association with the National AIDS Control Organisation, New Delhi.

Folk media & street theatre

We are empanelled with the Song & Drama Division of the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting. In the Thar, a message travels much further when it is sung and acted in the village square than when it is printed on a poster nobody reads.

Women's empowerment

Supporting women to take part in decisions about their own lives, their households and their villages — and to earn from the embroidery and block-print skills this district has always had in abundance.

Human rights education

Teaching people what their rights actually are is the quickest route out of exploitation. We work for a society of equal rights, especially for communities that have historically been kept furthest from them.

Traditional water harvesting

In a drought-prone district, the old answers were often the good ones. We promote the revival of tanka and other traditional water structures that stored the monsoon for the whole year.

Disability inclusion

Working for the empowerment of persons with disabilities — access to entitlements, to schemes, to schooling, and to a say in community life.

Credentials

Everything we do is done as a registered society, on the record, with the departments that oversee this kind of work.
