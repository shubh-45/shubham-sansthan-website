Our work

What we actually do in Barmer district

Long distances, scarce water, and communities that get reached last. Our programmes follow the shape of that problem.

Programmes

Nine areas of work

Some of these are formal programmes run with a government partner. Others are simply what needed doing, in a district where a lot needs doing.

Health

HIV & AIDS awareness

This is our longest-running programme and the one we are best known for. In association with the National AIDS Control Organisation, New Delhi, we have held HIV and AIDS awareness programmes across Barmer district continuously for more than seventeen years.

In a border district with heavy seasonal migration for work, this is not an abstract public-health topic. Plain, unembarrassed information — delivered in the local language, in places where people already gather — is the whole of the intervention.

Communication

Folk media, song & street theatre

We are registered with the Public Relations Bureau and empanelled with the Song & Drama Division of the Government of India's Ministry of Information & Broadcasting.

That empanelment describes our method as much as our credentials. In the Thar, a printed poster on a wall reaches very few people. A song, a nukkad natak in the village square, a performance that borrows the rhythm of the region's own musicians — that reaches everybody, including the people who cannot read the poster.

Entitlements

The Aadhaar promotion campaign

We worked on the Aadhaar promotion campaign, helping people across the district get enrolled and understand what the identity number is for.

In remote villages, an unenrolled family is a family cut off from rations, pensions, scholarships and bank accounts. Enrolment drives are unglamorous work with very direct consequences.

Emergency

Service through the COVID-19 pandemic

Through the coronavirus pandemic the Sansthan stayed in the field, carrying information, assistance and reassurance to families across Barmer at a time when very little else was moving.

Empowerment

Women's empowerment

Our mission commits us to taking an active part in the empowerment of women. In practice, in this district, that means two things at once.

The first is voice — supporting women to take part in decisions about their own lives, their households and their villages, and backing the women sarpanches and representatives who have begun to change what is expected of them.

The second is earning. Barmer's embroidery and block-print skills are world-famous and sit in the hands of thousands of rural women. Income from that craft has done more to shift household authority here than any lecture ever could.

Rights

Empowerment of persons with disabilities

We work for the empowerment of persons with disabilities: access to the certificates, schemes and pensions they are entitled to, access to schooling, and a real say in community life rather than a place at its edge.

Rights

Human rights education

Our mission places human rights education at the centre of the national effort to develop poor and excluded communities. Teaching people what their rights actually are is the fastest route out of exploitation — and the cheapest.

We work for a society of equal rights, with particular attention to the communities that have historically been kept furthest from them.

Environment

Traditional water harvesting

Barmer lives under the standing threat of drought. Long before piped supply, this district survived on the tanka — an underground tank that catches and stores the monsoon for the rest of the year — and on deep wells maintained by the whole village.

We promote the revival of these traditional water structures in drought-prone areas. The old answers here were engineered for exactly this climate, and abandoning them was expensive.

Livelihoods

Sustainable development & standard of living

We work on the issues tied to the sustainable development of society, and to raising the standard of living of people living in villages and in urban slum settlements — alongside the questions of information, technology, policy, resources and environment that decide whether any of it lasts.

Track record

How the work has grown

  1. The Sansthan is founded

    Subham Sansthan is established at Barmer in the year 2000, and registered under the Rajasthan Societies Registration Act, 1958.

  2. Formal registration completed

    Registration follows with the Government of India's Ministry of Information & Broadcasting, and with the National AIDS Control Organisation in New Delhi.

  3. Seventeen unbroken years of AIDS awareness

    HIV and AIDS awareness programmes run continuously across the district — the Sansthan's longest and most consistent commitment.

  4. The Aadhaar promotion campaign

    Enrolment and awareness work carried into villages across Barmer, connecting families to the entitlements the identity number unlocks.

  5. Service through the pandemic

    The Sansthan stays in the field through COVID-19, delivering information and assistance across the district.

  6. Recognised at district level, more than once

    Honoured repeatedly for notable contribution to social welfare work in Barmer.
